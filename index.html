Tanvir Ahmed.

Machine learning for security,
and evaluation that holds up.

I am a computer science graduate and independent researcher. My work covers network security, medical image analysis and quantum machine learning, and in all three I focus on whether the results hold up.

Four of my papers were accepted in 2026. One of them shows that cutting a feature set can leave overall accuracy almost untouched while making a rare attack class far harder to detect, and gives a way to find the harmed classes and repair the subset.

I hold a BSc in Computer Science from Independent University, Bangladesh, and work independently in Boca Raton, Florida. Across everything I do, the recurring question is whether a reported number would survive someone checking it.

02 / ResearchWhat I am working out

01

Can a security model run on the user's own machine?

A vision-language model and an LLM, both 4-bit quantized, reading a page locally instead of sending the URL to someone else's blocklist.

02

Which classes does feature selection leave behind?

Aggregate accuracy holds steady while a rare attack class quietly loses most of its detectability. Finding those classes, and putting the information back.

DARE-FS ↓
03

What changes at an equal parameter budget?

Quantum kernels, variational circuits and QUBO optimization, measured against classical methods given the same number of parameters rather than a favourable comparison.
